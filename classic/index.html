How to use this page

Which spare parts could stop the plant - and what can stand in for them. Everything runs on this computer.

Overview opens first: one number (the expected yearly cost of stoppages from missing parts), the parts that carry that risk, and three lists - Act now, Out of stock but covered, Registered twice. Click any part: the right-hand panel explains it and says what to do.

The other tabs are tools, one per screen:

  • Ask - type a need in plain words ("P-101 stops tomorrow - what do I need?").
  • What-if - fix the weak spots, or test a disruption (a supplier country, a shipping delay, a warehouse).
  • Warnings - which parts will run out, and the last day to order.
  • Warehouses - Yanbu next to the other sites, and the unused stock that could help.
  • Value - what the approach is worth, with your own numbers.
  • Catalogue - parts registered twice, and what the field says about replacements.
  • Network - for engineers: every part linked to the job it does.
Reading the Network tab
  • Each dot is a spare part. Red = vital (the plant stops without it), orange = essential, grey = nice to have.
  • Bigger dot = more money at risk if that part is not available when needed.
  • Teal labels are part families grouped by the job they do (seal, gasket, bearing...). We ask "which part can do this job?", not "do we have this exact part number?".
  • Blue dashed arrow = this part can safely replace the other one (same job, equal or better spec).
  • Thick orange line = the same part registered twice under different numbers - stock we did not know we had.
  • Green diamonds are other warehouses. A solid green line = that warehouse holds the part unused, so it can be transferred.
  • Click any dot - the right-hand panel explains it in plain words and says what to do.
