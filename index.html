How to use this page

When a part is missing, the Brain finds what else can do its job - where it is, and the fastest, cheapest and safest way to get it.

Find opens first: type what you need - a part number or the job in your own words ("seal, Viton, 50 mm, class 300"). Every part that can do it lights up, then the alternatives tree grows: branch length = days, colour = how sure we are (Proven used before, Tested approved or same spec, Theoretical by the rules).

  • Coverage - which units have an alternative for their parts, and the parts running short with the alternative for each.
  • Catalogue - same part under several numbers, fewer variants, 3D-print shortlist, what the field says.
  • Value - warehouses side by side and what alternatives are worth.
  • Network - for engineers: every part linked to the job it does.

No alternative → the buying decision belongs to Procurement Copilot. Risk diagnosis is SPOF's; crises are Resilience Shield's. Advisory only - a person approves every action.

Reading the Network tab
  • Each dot is a spare part. Red = vital (the plant stops without it), orange = essential, grey = nice to have.
  • Bigger dot = more alternatives for that part.
  • Teal labels are part families grouped by the job they do (seal, gasket, bearing...). We ask "which part can do this job?", not "do we have this exact part number?".
  • Blue dashed arrow = this part can safely replace the other one (same job, equal or better spec).
  • Thick orange line = the same part registered twice under different numbers - stock we did not know we had.
  • Green diamonds are other warehouses. A solid green line = that warehouse holds the part unused, so it can be transferred.
  • Click any dot - the right-hand panel explains it in plain words and says what to do.
